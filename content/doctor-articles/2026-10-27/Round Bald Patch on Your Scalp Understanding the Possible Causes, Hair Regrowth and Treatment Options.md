# Round Bald Patch on Your Scalp? Understanding the Possible Causes, Hair Regrowth and Treatment Options

Noticing a round bald patch on your scalp can be surprising, especially when there were no obvious signs of hair loss beforehand.

Sometimes, a person discovers a small patch while combing their hair or looking in the mirror. In other cases, a barber, friend or family member notices it first.

This can raise several questions:

- Why has a bald patch appeared?
- Will it become larger?
- Can the hair grow back naturally?
- Is the hair loss permanent?
- Does it require medical treatment?

A round bald patch does not always mean permanent hair loss. Several conditions can produce localized hair loss, and their causes and treatments differ.

Understanding these differences can help you make informed decisions.

## What Causes a Round Bald Patch on the Scalp?

One possible cause is **alopecia areata**, an autoimmune condition associated with localized hair loss.

In alopecia areata, the immune system mistakenly targets hair follicles and interferes with normal hair growth.

The condition commonly causes smooth, round or oval areas of hair loss. It may affect the scalp, beard, eyebrows or other areas of the body.

Some people develop a single small patch. Others experience multiple patches or more extensive hair loss.

### What does medical research say?

A 2026 expert consensus published in the *International Journal of Dermatology*, involving dermatologists from several Asia-Pacific countries including India, describes alopecia areata as a condition with considerable differences in presentation, severity and treatment needs.

The consensus emphasizes individualized assessment rather than assuming that every person with alopecia areata has the same clinical course.

[Research reference: Sinclair et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41699409/)

Importantly, **not every round bald patch is alopecia areata**.

Other possible causes include:

**Fungal scalp infections:** Certain fungal infections can cause localized hair loss, sometimes accompanied by itching, scaling or broken hairs.

**Traction alopecia:** Repeated pulling from tight hairstyles can contribute to hair loss, especially near the hairline.

**Hair-pulling disorders:** Repeated pulling of hair may produce irregular patches with hairs of different lengths.

**Scarring alopecia:** Certain inflammatory scalp conditions can damage hair follicles and may cause permanent hair loss.

These conditions can resemble one another, but they may require different approaches to diagnosis and treatment.

## How Is Patchy Hair Loss Different From Normal Hair Shedding?

Losing some hair during daily activities is part of the normal hair-growth cycle.

However, increased shedding and localized bald patches are not necessarily the same problem.

Diffuse shedding usually involves hair falling from different areas of the scalp, sometimes causing an overall reduction in density.

Alopecia areata often causes more clearly defined patches of missing hair.

For example, a person experiencing increased shedding may notice additional hairs while washing or combing. Someone with patchy alopecia areata may instead notice a distinct area where hair is missing.

These patterns are useful clues, but they cannot establish a diagnosis on their own.

A person may also experience more than one type of hair loss at the same time.

## Can Hair Grow Back Naturally in a Bald Patch?

Hair regrowth is possible in alopecia areata.

Because alopecia areata is generally a non-scarring form of hair loss, affected follicles may retain the ability to produce hair.

Some people experience spontaneous regrowth, meaning hair returns without active medical treatment.

However, recovery is not predictable.

Hair may regrow in one area while another patch develops. Some individuals experience repeated episodes, while others have persistent hair loss.

### What does medical research say?

A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis published in *Acta Dermato-Venereologica* examined hair regrowth among participants in the placebo groups of clinical trials involving alopecia areata.

The researchers observed that regrowth without active treatment was possible, but substantial improvement was uncommon among the studied populations, particularly those with extensive disease.

The findings also highlighted the difficulty of predicting natural recovery because patients differ in disease severity and duration.

[Research reference: Jastrząb-Miśkiewicz et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41147234/)

These findings should not be used to predict what will happen to a particular person with a small bald patch.

**The important point is that regrowth is possible, but it cannot be guaranteed.**

## Can Alopecia Areata Return After Hair Regrowth?

Yes. Alopecia areata can follow a recurring pattern.

Some people experience one episode of patchy hair loss and subsequently maintain their regrowth.

Others develop new episodes affecting the same area or different parts of the scalp.

The timing and extent of recurrence vary considerably.

Clinical research recognizes that the natural course of alopecia areata is unpredictable.

Even when treatment is followed by successful regrowth, future episodes may still occur.

This does not necessarily mean that the earlier treatment was unsuccessful or inappropriate.

It reflects the fact that alopecia areata can remain an active or recurring condition.

## Is Stress Responsible for Round Bald Patches?

Stress and hair loss are frequently discussed together, but their relationship is complex.

Psychological or physical stress may be associated with certain forms of hair loss and may influence alopecia areata in some individuals.

However, alopecia areata involves immune-system activity and other biological factors.

Stress alone cannot explain every case.

Another condition, called telogen effluvium, may occur following illnesses, significant physical stress or other health-related changes. It typically causes more widespread shedding rather than clearly defined round patches.

A bald patch should therefore not automatically be attributed to stress.

## Can Vitamin Deficiencies Contribute to Hair Loss?

Nutritional deficiencies may contribute to some forms of hair loss.

However, the presence of a deficiency does not necessarily explain the development of a circular bald patch.

### What does medical research say?

A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis published in *Frontiers in Nutrition* examined vitamin D levels among people with different hair-loss conditions.

The review identified an association between alopecia areata and lower vitamin D levels.

However, an association does not establish that vitamin D deficiency directly causes alopecia areata.

The researchers also identified the need for further investigation into whether vitamin D supplementation improves hair outcomes.

[Research reference: Yongpisarn et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39416654/)

Correcting a confirmed nutritional deficiency may be important for overall health, but vitamin supplements should not be regarded as a universal treatment for patchy baldness.

Whether blood tests are appropriate depends on individual symptoms, medical history and examination findings.

## How Is the Cause of a Bald Patch Diagnosed?

A medical evaluation generally begins with understanding the history of hair loss.

Relevant questions may include:

- When was the patch first noticed?
- Has its size changed?
- Have additional patches appeared?
- Is there itching, scaling, redness or discomfort?
- Has similar hair loss occurred before?
- Are there other relevant medical conditions or symptoms?

A physical examination can provide important information about the pattern of hair loss and condition of the scalp.

A dermatologist may use **trichoscopy**, a method of examining the hair and scalp with magnification, to look for features that help distinguish different hair-loss conditions.

### What does medical research say?

The 2026 Asia-Pacific expert consensus identifies trichoscopy as an important tool for evaluating and monitoring alopecia areata.

It also indicates that a scalp biopsy is generally reserved for situations where the underlying diagnosis remains uncertain.

The updated British Association of Dermatologists guideline similarly emphasizes clinical assessment and investigations selected according to individual findings.

[Asia-Pacific expert consensus, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41699409/)

[British Association of Dermatologists living guideline, 2025 edition](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41234147/)

This means that not every bald patch requires extensive testing.

The need for further investigation depends on the clinical presentation.

## What Treatment Options Are Available for Alopecia Areata?

Treatment decisions depend on several factors, including the diagnosis, extent of hair loss, age, disease activity, medical history and individual preferences.

There is no single treatment suitable for every person.

### Observation and monitoring

In selected cases of limited alopecia areata, observation may be considered because some people experience spontaneous regrowth.

However, monitoring should not replace appropriate assessment, especially when hair loss is progressing.

### Topical medicines

Prescription topical corticosteroids may be considered for some patients with alopecia areata.

These medicines are intended to address inflammation associated with the condition.

Their suitability depends on individual circumstances.

### Corticosteroid injections

For certain patients with limited patchy alopecia areata, a dermatologist may consider corticosteroid injections into affected areas.

Potential benefits, limitations and adverse effects should be discussed before treatment.

### Other medical treatments

Additional treatment options are available for some patients, particularly those with more extensive disease.

These may include specialist-supervised systemic medicines.

Such treatments require consideration of possible adverse effects, medical history, monitoring requirements and applicable regulatory approvals.

### What does medical research say?

The updated British Association of Dermatologists guideline reviews different treatment approaches according to disease severity and individual needs.

It also recognizes the importance of discussing treatment choices and expectations with patients.

[Research guideline: Harries et al., 2025 edition](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41234147/)

**No treatment can guarantee permanent hair regrowth for every person with alopecia areata.**

Prescription medicines should only be used as directed by an appropriately qualified medical professional.

## Why Might a Bald Patch Continue to Grow Despite Treatment?

Hair-loss conditions vary in their course and response to treatment.

A patch may remain unchanged, improve gradually or continue to enlarge.

Possible reasons include ongoing disease activity, differences in individual treatment response or an underlying diagnosis that requires reconsideration.

Visible regrowth can also take time.

If a patch continues to expand or new patches appear, reassessment may help determine whether the diagnosis or treatment plan needs to be reviewed.

Treatment outcomes should be judged in the context of the specific condition rather than assuming that every type of hair loss should respond in the same way.

## Can Home Remedies Help Round Bald Patches?

Hair oils, onion juice, garlic and other home remedies are frequently discussed in relation to hair loss.

However, reliable clinical evidence does not establish these remedies as consistent treatments for alopecia areata.

Some preparations may irritate the scalp or cause allergic reactions.

Hair oils may help condition the hair shaft, but this should not be confused with treating an underlying autoimmune or inflammatory disorder.

Using unverified remedies may also delay the diagnosis of conditions that require specific medical care.

Avoid applying harsh substances to areas of hair loss or using prescription medicines without appropriate guidance.

## Can Patchy Hair Loss Affect Confidence and Emotional Well-Being?

Hair loss can affect more than physical appearance.

Some people become concerned about visible patches while attending work, studying, socializing or appearing in photographs.

Others worry about whether the condition will progress.

These reactions differ between individuals.

### What does medical research say?

A systematic review and meta-analysis published in *Frontiers in Medicine* in 2022 examined psychological well-being and quality of life among people with alopecia areata.

The researchers found that adults with alopecia areata reported greater symptoms of anxiety and depression than healthy control groups, on average.

The findings also indicated that alopecia areata can negatively affect quality of life.

[Research reference: van Dalen et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36523776/)

These findings do not mean that everyone with alopecia areata experiences psychological difficulties.

They show that the emotional and social effects of hair loss are important considerations alongside its physical symptoms.

## When Should a Bald Patch Be Medically Evaluated?

Consider arranging a medical assessment if you notice an unexplained bald patch, particularly when:

- The patch is increasing in size.
- Additional bald areas are appearing.
- Hair loss involves the eyebrows, eyelashes or beard.
- The affected area is itchy, painful, red or scaly.
- Hair loss repeatedly returns.
- The skin appears inflamed or scarred.

A scalp area with significant pain, swelling, pus or marked inflammation requires prompt medical attention because some infections and inflammatory conditions can lead to complications.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is every round bald patch caused by alopecia areata?

No. Several conditions, including fungal infections and certain inflammatory disorders, can cause localized hair loss.

### Can hair grow back without treatment?

Yes, some people with alopecia areata experience spontaneous regrowth. However, recovery varies, and not every case improves without treatment.

### Is alopecia areata contagious?

No. Alopecia areata is an autoimmune condition and cannot spread from one person to another through ordinary contact. Some fungal infections that cause patchy hair loss can be contagious.

### Can alopecia areata become permanent?

Alopecia areata is generally non-scarring, meaning affected follicles may retain the ability to regrow hair. However, some people experience prolonged or extensive hair loss. Other causes of patchy hair loss may produce permanent follicle damage.

### Does everyone need steroid injections?

No. Treatment choices depend on several factors, including diagnosis, severity, age and individual medical circumstances.

### Can vitamins cure alopecia areata?

Current evidence does not establish vitamin supplementation as a universal cure for alopecia areata.

### Will shaving the head help the hair grow back?

Shaving does not change the autoimmune process associated with alopecia areata or restore hair-follicle activity. It is generally a cosmetic choice rather than a treatment.

## Conclusion

A round bald patch on the scalp can develop for several reasons, and its appearance alone does not establish the cause.

Alopecia areata is one possible explanation, but fungal infections, inflammatory conditions and other forms of localized hair loss may produce similar symptoms.

Research shows that hair regrowth is possible in some cases, although recovery and recurrence vary between individuals.

Different conditions also require different approaches to diagnosis and treatment.

Understanding the cause, considering the available evidence and maintaining realistic expectations can help people make better-informed decisions about their hair and scalp health.

**The most important first step is identifying the underlying condition rather than assuming that every bald patch needs the same treatment.**
