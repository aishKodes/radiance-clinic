# Tried Multiple Hair Fall and Dandruff Treatments but Still Struggling? Understanding the Root Cause May Be the Missing Step

**Why hair thinning, receding hairlines and recurring dandruff may continue despite previous treatments — and what scientific research tells us.**

Hair thinning, a receding hairline and recurring dandruff can be frustrating, particularly when different treatments have already been tried without the expected improvement.

Some people notice more hair falling during washing or combing. Others observe gradual thinning around the temples or crown. Some experience repeated scalp itching and flaking, even after using products that previously provided relief.

A shampoo may improve dandruff temporarily, but the flakes return. Hair shedding may decrease, yet the lost density does not seem to recover. Different products or treatments may produce different results, leaving several questions unanswered.

- Why is my hair still thinning despite previous treatment?
- Why does dandruff return after temporarily improving?
- Is hair loss hereditary, or could another condition be contributing?
- Why did a treatment help someone else but not me?
- Can lost hair density recover?
- Could two different conditions be affecting my hair and scalp simultaneously?

These questions highlight an important distinction: **similar symptoms do not necessarily have the same underlying cause.**

Hair shedding, progressive hair loss, hair breakage and inflammatory scalp disorders involve different biological processes. Understanding these differences may help explain why some treatments produce limited results or why symptoms return.

## 1. Why Might Hair Problems Continue Despite Previous Treatment?

People experiencing persistent hair or scalp problems may have tried shampoos, oils, supplements, topical preparations or prescribed treatments.

An unsatisfactory result does not necessarily mean that every previous treatment was inappropriate.

Several factors can affect treatment response:

- The underlying condition may not have been fully identified.
- More than one condition may be contributing to the symptoms.
- The treatment may not have been suitable for the particular condition.
- The duration or consistency of treatment may have been insufficient.
- The condition may naturally follow a recurring or progressive course.
- The expected result may differ from what a treatment can realistically achieve.

For example, reducing excessive shedding and increasing overall hair density are not identical outcomes.

A treatment that helps reduce shedding may not reverse thinning caused by a different process.

Similarly, a treatment that controls scalp flaking may not permanently prevent future episodes.

Understanding the original diagnosis, treatment duration and response can help clarify what may have happened.

## 2. Hair Fall Is a Symptom, Not a Single Condition

Hair loss can occur for several reasons.

Some conditions affect the normal hair-growth cycle. Others progressively reduce the thickness of individual hairs. Certain scalp diseases may cause inflammation or damage affecting hair growth.

### Androgenetic Alopecia

Also called pattern hair loss, androgenetic alopecia is associated with genetic susceptibility and hormonal influences.

It commonly causes gradual thinning in characteristic patterns, such as recession at the temples, thinning at the crown or widening of the central parting.

### Telogen Effluvium

Telogen effluvium is a form of excessive hair shedding that can develop after physiological stressors, illness, nutritional problems or other triggers.

The shedding is often diffuse rather than concentrated in one particular area.

### Hair Breakage

Hair may also appear thinner because strands break along their length.

Heat styling, chemical processing, friction and mechanical damage can contribute to breakage.

This differs from hair being shed from the follicle.

### Other Causes

Certain nutritional deficiencies, thyroid conditions, inflammatory disorders and infections can also contribute to hair loss.

Because these conditions can overlap, the pattern of hair loss and the individual's history are important when considering possible explanations.

**What does research say?**

A review titled *Telogen Effluvium: A Review*, published in the *Journal of Clinical and Diagnostic Research* in 2015, describes how excessive shedding often becomes noticeable approximately two to three months after a triggering event.

The review highlights the importance of medical history when investigating diffuse hair shedding. [1]

This delayed response helps explain why the cause of shedding may not always be immediately apparent.

## 3. Why Does the Hairline Sometimes Continue to Recede?

A receding hairline may develop gradually over several years.

Some individuals initially notice thinning around the temples, while others experience reduced density around the crown.

When these changes result from androgenetic alopecia, a process called follicular miniaturisation is involved.

During this process, susceptible hair follicles gradually produce finer and shorter hairs.

Over time, this may make parts of the scalp more visible.

**What does research say?**

A 2023 review titled *Androgenetic Alopecia: An Update*, published in *JAAD International*, discusses the roles of genetic predisposition and androgen sensitivity in progressive follicular miniaturisation. [2]

This process is different from ordinary hair-shaft damage.

Products designed mainly for cleansing, conditioning or improving hair appearance should therefore not be assumed to reverse hereditary pattern hair loss.

Medical treatments may help some individuals slow progression or improve density, but responses vary.

Not every receding hairline follows the same progression, and not everyone experiences complete baldness.

## 4. Why Can Hair Shedding Decrease While Hair Density Remains Low?

Some people notice that fewer hairs are falling each day, yet their hair still looks thinner than before.

This can occur for several reasons.

Hair growth follows a biological cycle, and visible changes in density may take time.

After an episode of temporary shedding, new hair may need several months to grow long enough to contribute noticeably to overall fullness.

In other cases, temporary shedding may occur alongside pattern hair loss.

A reduction in shedding does not necessarily mean that follicular miniaturisation has stopped.

Hair breakage and changes in strand thickness may also influence how full the hair appears.

The 2015 review on telogen effluvium describes how recovery can occur gradually after a contributing trigger has resolved. [1]

The distinction between the number of hairs being shed and the overall appearance of hair density is therefore important.

## 5. Why Does Dandruff Sometimes Return After Treatment?

Dandruff may cause visible flakes, itching and scalp discomfort.

Some individuals experience improvement with appropriate products but notice the symptoms returning later.

One possible explanation is seborrheic dermatitis.

Seborrheic dermatitis is a common inflammatory skin condition that often affects the scalp and other areas rich in sebaceous glands.

Its development involves several factors, including skin oils, immune responses and microorganisms such as Malassezia yeasts.

The condition often follows a chronic or relapsing course.

**What does research say?**

A 2022 review, *An Overview of the Diagnosis and Management of Seborrheic Dermatitis*, published in *Clinical, Cosmetic and Investigational Dermatology*, describes seborrheic dermatitis as a chronic inflammatory condition in which symptoms can recur after improvement. [3]

Because recurrence is part of the condition's natural history, some individuals may require an ongoing management approach.

However, recurring scalp flaking should not automatically be assumed to be seborrheic dermatitis. Other disorders can produce similar symptoms.

## 6. Not Every Flaky or Itchy Scalp Has the Same Cause

Scalp flaking and itching can occur in several conditions.

**Seborrheic dermatitis** may produce white or yellowish flakes with varying degrees of itching and inflammation.

**Scalp psoriasis** may produce thicker, more clearly defined scaly patches.

**Contact dermatitis** can occur when the skin reacts to an irritating substance or allergen, including certain ingredients in hair products.

**Fungal scalp infections**, such as tinea capitis, may produce scaling, broken hairs or localised hair loss.

These conditions are not necessarily distinguishable by symptoms alone.

Their management may also differ.

When symptoms persist, become painful or fail to respond as expected, reconsidering the diagnosis may be appropriate.

Clinical history and examination are often central to distinguishing inflammatory scalp conditions. Additional testing is reserved for situations where it may help clarify the diagnosis. [3]

## 7. Are Dandruff and Progressive Hair Thinning Connected?

Some individuals experience scalp flaking and hair thinning at the same time.

This can raise the question of whether dandruff is responsible for their hair loss.

The answer depends on the underlying conditions.

Scalp inflammation and repeated scratching can sometimes contribute to hair shedding or breakage.

However, ordinary dandruff is not generally considered a direct cause of hereditary pattern baldness.

An individual can also have more than one hair or scalp condition simultaneously.

For example, androgenetic alopecia and seborrheic dermatitis may occur in the same person.

The mechanisms of these conditions differ: androgenetic alopecia involves follicular miniaturisation, while seborrheic dermatitis is an inflammatory scalp disorder. [2, 3]

Managing scalp inflammation may improve itching and flaking without necessarily reversing thinning caused by another condition.

This is why the relationship between scalp symptoms and changes in hair density should not be assumed without assessment.

## 8. Can Water, Weather or Changes in Routine Affect Hair?

Some people notice changes in hair texture or scalp comfort after relocating or changing their daily routine.

Humidity, washing frequency, styling practices and water characteristics may influence how hair feels and behaves.

Hair may become more difficult to manage, appear frizzy or experience increased breakage under certain conditions.

However, observing hair shedding after an environmental change does not establish that the environment caused the shedding.

Other factors, including illness, changes in nutrition, medications and existing hair-loss conditions, may also be relevant.

It is useful to distinguish changes in hair texture or breakage from progressive hair loss arising at the follicle.

Environmental explanations should be considered as possibilities rather than assumed to be the primary cause.

## 9. Why Can Hair Loss Affect Emotional Well-Being?

Changes in hair appearance can affect individuals differently.

Some people are relatively unconcerned about thinning hair, while others experience frustration, embarrassment or reduced confidence.

The emotional impact may depend on personal expectations, social circumstances and the extent to which hair appearance matters to an individual.

**What does research say?**

A 2021 systematic review and meta-analysis published in *JAMA Dermatology* examined 41 studies involving 7,995 individuals with androgenetic alopecia.

The researchers found an association between androgenetic alopecia and impairment in health-related quality of life, particularly its emotional dimension. [4]

However, the pooled findings did not demonstrate an association with depressive symptoms, and individual experiences varied.

These findings indicate that the emotional effects of hair loss can be relevant to a person's overall well-being, without implying that everyone with hair loss experiences psychological distress.

## 10. What Does Assessment of Hair and Scalp Problems Involve?

The evaluation of hair and scalp symptoms depends on the individual's history, examination findings and suspected condition.

### Medical and Hair History

Relevant information may include:

- When the symptoms began
- Whether the changes occurred suddenly or gradually
- The pattern of hair thinning or shedding
- Family history of hair loss
- Recent illnesses or medication changes
- Nutritional or other health concerns
- Previous treatments and their results

### Hair and Scalp Examination

Clinical examination may help identify patterns of thinning, inflammation, scaling, breakage or other abnormalities.

Dermatoscopy or trichoscopy may provide additional information when indicated.

### Further Investigations

Certain circumstances may justify blood tests, fungal testing or other investigations.

Not every person experiencing hair loss requires the same tests.

The decision depends on the clinical findings and the possible underlying conditions.

### Reviewing Treatment Response

Some hair and scalp conditions require follow-up because changes develop gradually.

Monitoring may involve symptoms, clinical findings and, when appropriate, photographs taken under consistent conditions.

A clear diagnosis is not always possible immediately. Sometimes several contributing factors need to be considered.

## 11. Why Do Treatment Expectations Matter?

Different hair and scalp conditions have different natural histories.

Some episodes of excessive shedding improve after the contributing trigger resolves.

Pattern hair loss may progress over time and can require ongoing management.

Seborrheic dermatitis may improve with treatment but remain susceptible to future flare-ups.

Therefore, improvement does not always mean complete restoration or permanent disappearance of symptoms.

Possible treatment goals may include reduced shedding, stabilisation of hair density, relief from inflammation or better control of recurring scalp symptoms.

The outcome depends on the condition, individual characteristics and treatment response.

Research reviews of androgenetic alopecia, telogen effluvium and seborrheic dermatitis describe different mechanisms and different expectations for management. [1, 2, 3]

Understanding these differences can help individuals interpret their progress more accurately.

## 12. When Is Medical Evaluation Important?

Mild hair shedding and occasional scalp flaking may occur without indicating a serious underlying condition.

However, some changes merit professional evaluation.

These include:

- Gradually increasing hairline recession or crown thinning
- A widening parting or noticeable reduction in hair density
- Sudden or unusually heavy shedding
- Distinct patches of hair loss
- Persistent itching or significant scalp inflammation
- Scalp flaking that continues despite appropriate routine care
- Broken hairs accompanied by scaling or irritation
- Changes suggesting possible scarring of the scalp

Painful swelling, pus, rapidly progressing patches of hair loss or signs of infection require prompt medical attention.

The purpose of evaluation is to determine whether a treatable condition is present and what management, if any, is appropriate.

## Conclusion: Understanding the Problem Before Choosing the Next Treatment

Hair thinning, progressive hairline recession and recurring scalp flaking can have several explanations.

Although these symptoms may appear similar, the underlying biological processes are not always the same.

Temporary excessive shedding differs from hereditary pattern hair loss.

Hair breakage differs from follicular hair loss.

Recurring dandruff may be associated with chronic scalp inflammation rather than a single temporary episode.

In some individuals, multiple conditions may occur together.

Scientific research supports evaluating the pattern, history and characteristics of hair and scalp symptoms when considering possible causes.

Previous treatment responses can provide useful information, but they should be interpreted in the context of the suspected condition.

Understanding the possible causes, natural progression and limitations of treatment can support more informed decisions about hair and scalp health.

**Before trying another treatment, understanding why the problem is happening may be an important first step.**
